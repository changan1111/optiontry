Learn: key terms

Models: when to use which

Black–Scholes–Merton prices a European option on a spot price S with rate r and dividend yield q. Black-76 prices it on a futures price F, so spot and dividends are not needed. Nifty options are settled against the index at expiry and traded alongside futures, so Black-76 on the matching-expiry future is the usual market-style choice; spot Black–Scholes suits learning and index-level analysis.

Options basics

A call gains when the underlying rises, a put when it falls. The strike K is the agreed level. Intrinsic value is the payoff if exercised now; time value is the rest of the premium. Time value shrinks toward expiry (time decay). Higher implied volatility (IV) raises premiums because larger moves become more likely. The risk-free rate and dividend yield shift the forward price and the discounting. A theoretical premium comes from the model; a market premium is what actually traded and can differ.

Greeks (sensitivities, not outcomes)

Delta: premium change per 1 point of the underlying. Gamma: change in delta per 1 point. Theta: premium change per calendar day. Vega: premium change per 1 IV percentage point. Rho: premium change per 1 percentage point of rate. They are local estimates; large moves make them less accurate. At expiry or with zero volatility some are undefined (shown as n/a); where the limit is 0 the page says so.

Time convention and limits

T = calendar days ÷ 365 as a date-only approximation. No exchange calendar, holidays, or exact expiry time (15:30 IST) are modelled. 0 days means payoff at expiry. IV is shown in %; the engine uses decimals. Supported ranges: days 0–3650, IV 0–1000%, rate −10 to 100%, yield −10 to 50%. European exercise, constant rate, yield and volatility. IV rank is not calculated because no volatility history is stored. Educational only; this is not trading advice.

The formulas

d1 = [ln(S/K) + (r − q + σ²/2)·T] / (σ√T), d2 = d1 − σ√T.
Call = S·e−qT·N(d1) − K·e−rT·N(d2). Put = K·e−rT·N(−d2) − S·e−qT·N(−d1).
T = days ÷ 365. Implied volatility has no closed form: the page tries σ values (bisection) until the model price equals the market price. Newton-Raphson does the same faster: σ ← σ − (model − market) ÷ vega.
1σ expected move = S·σ·√T. Chance of finishing in the money = N(d2) for a call, N(−d2) for a put. Breakeven = strike ± premium.
IV rank is explanatory only (not calculated here, since it needs a 1-year IV high and low): (IV − low) ÷ (high − low). It does not by itself make selling or buying profitable.
Educational tool, not trading advice. Real prices also reflect bid/ask, skew and the futures basis.
